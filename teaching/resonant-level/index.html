Resonant-level electronic friction

Compare a known continuum result with different Gaussian broadening expressions.

Parameters

μ = 0. One spin channel. Γ is a physical width; σ is numerical smearing. Values of η/ℏ are in Å⁻².

1 · Model

One orbital coupled to a metal

Moving x shifts the orbital energy. Electrons are noninteracting; the metal has a wide flat band and constant coupling.

εd(x)=ε0+gx,ρd(ε)=1πΓ2(ε−εd)2+(Γ2)2

ρd integrates to one over all energies. Only part of its tails is shown.

Expressions used in the comparison

For a positive transition Δ, including its reverse gives the kernels below. Both multiply the same positive-transition occupation difference and squared coupling.

K1A(E,Δ)=Gσ(E−Δ)−Gσ(E+Δ)E
K1B(E,Δ)=Gσ(E−Δ)+Gσ(E+Δ)Δ

1A broadens the dissipative response before dividing by E. 1B broadens friction directly. At E = 0, 1A uses its analytic limit. Both approach the continuum result as σ tends to zero.

Positive-only 1B retains Gσ(E − Δ)/Δ. Normalised 1B divides that kernel by A(Δ) = [1 + erf(Δ/(√2σ))]/2. This restores each peak’s positive-energy area, not necessarily the correct spectrum.

These plots broaden the continuum transition distribution. They do not model a finite bath or a discrete electronic mesh.

Reference and conventions

Dou and Subotnik (2016), Electronic friction near metal surfaces: spectral function and equilibrium friction, specialised here to constant Γ. Their spectral function A = 2πρd. The finite-frequency reference is the equilibrium linear response of this same model.

η multiplies nuclear velocity in the friction force. Divide by nuclear mass to obtain a damping rate. Two identical independent spin channels double all friction values. Numerical integrals resolve Γ and kBT; Gaussian convolution extends eight σ beyond the plotted energy range.
