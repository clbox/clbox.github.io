The model and the minimal equations

Let S(Δ) be the positive-frequency part of a real, even friction response. Each electronic transition at +Δ has an equal-weight partner at −Δ. G is a unit-area Gaussian of standard deviation σ; Φ is the standard normal cumulative distribution.

Positive only: Λ(E) = ∫₀∞ S(Δ) G(E − Δ) dΔ
Peak normalized: Λ(E) = ∫₀∞ S(Δ) G(E − Δ) / Φ(Δ/σ) dΔ
With partners: Λ(E) = ∫₀∞ S(Δ) [G(E − Δ) + G(E + Δ)] dΔ

For S(Δ) = Λ₀, the zero-frequency results are Λ₀/2, Λ₀ ln(2), and Λ₀. Peak normalization restores the positive-energy area of each peak, but does not preserve a constant spectrum. The mirrored treatment preserves the flat spectrum at every energy.

For a non-flat spectrum, even full Gaussian convolution can change its shape. The dashed curve is the unbroadened input, not a curve that every finite-width result must match. All energies here are in eV and friction is in arbitrary units.

The input extends beyond the plotted window: flat remains flat, the peak returns to its background, the gap rises smoothly to its background, and the rising preset is S(Δ) = background × (1 + Δ / 1 eV). Numerical integration extends at least eight Gaussian widths past the visible positive-energy range.

This model concerns the real single-delta friction response with the occupation difference divided by the electronic energy difference, at q = 0. It does not describe diagonal/degenerate-state terms or prescribe how to broaden phonon peaks in α²F. A finite list of electronic transitions also requires separate sampling convergence.

Reference: Box, Stark & Maurer (2023), Eq. (14). The boundary examples are direct Gaussian-convolution calculations.
